Horror · Holiday watchlist

The Best Thanksgiving Horror Movies, Ranked

By Melody · November 19, 2026

Christmas has a whole shelf of horror. Halloween owns October. Thanksgiving? It gets a handful of slashers, a killer turkey or two, and one very uncomfortable family dinner. That's exactly why the list is fun: you can actually watch all of it over the long weekend.

Here are the best Thanksgiving horror movies, ranked from "put this on right after pie" down to "only if everyone's had enough wine to laugh at a turkey puppet." Almost every pick is set on or around Thanksgiving (the one that isn't is a turkey monster, so it counts), plus one bonus that isn't horror but earns its seat at the table.

  1. Thanksgiving (2023)
  2. Blood Rage (1987)
  3. The Humans (2021)
  4. Black Friday (2021)
  5. Home Sweet Home (1981)
  6. ThanksKilling (2008)
  7. Blood Freak (1972)
  8. Bonus: Addams Family Values (1993)

1. Thanksgiving (2023)

Director: Eli Roth · Starring Patrick Dempsey, Nell Verlaque, Addison Rae

The obvious number one, and it earns it. Eli Roth first teased this as a fake trailer in Grindhouse back in 2007, and sixteen years later he actually made the movie. A Black Friday sale at the RightMart store in Plymouth, Massachusetts turns into a deadly stampede. One year later, someone in a John Carver pilgrim mask starts picking off the people involved, with Patrick Dempsey as the sheriff trying to keep up.

It's a proper whodunit slasher in the Scream tradition: a masked killer, a group of suspects, and a holiday theme pushed as far as it will go. The kills are creative, the town feels real, and it's the rare modern slasher that knows when to be funny. Roth has announced a sequel, so watch this one now and get ahead of it.

2. Blood Rage (1987)

Director: John Grissmer · Starring Mark Soper, Louise Lasser · Also released as Nightmare at Shadow Woods

The cult classic of Thanksgiving horror. Twin brothers Todd and Terry (both played by Mark Soper) are kids when a killing at a drive-in gets pinned on Todd, and he's locked away. Ten years later, on Thanksgiving, Todd escapes, and the real trouble starts back home.

It's pure 80s slasher: practical gore, big hair, and Louise Lasser as the twins' mom giving a performance so unhinged it becomes the main event. Look up the cranberry sauce line before you watch and you'll know exactly what kind of night you're in for. If you love the 80s side of horror, this is the one to hunt down.

3. The Humans (2021)

Director: Stephen Karam · A24 · Starring Richard Jenkins, Amy Schumer, Steven Yeun, Beanie Feldstein, June Squibb

No masked killer here. Instead, a family sits down to Thanksgiving dinner in a run-down Manhattan apartment, and the building itself seems to be closing in on them: strange thuds from upstairs, lights dying one by one, stains spreading across the walls. Stephen Karam adapted his own Tony-winning play, and A24 put it out the day before Thanksgiving.

Critics still argue about whether it counts as horror. My take: it absolutely does. The dread is slow, quiet and real, and the scariest stuff is what this family won't say to each other. Put it on when you want a horror movie that gets under your skin instead of jumping out at you.

4. Black Friday (2021)

Director: Casey Tebo · Starring Bruce Campbell, Devon Sawa, Ivana Baquero, Michael Jai White

A horror comedy for anyone who has ever worked retail on Thanksgiving night. A toy store crew opens at midnight for the year's busiest shopping day, and the doorbusters turn into actual monsters. Bruce Campbell plays the store manager and Devon Sawa plays the worn-out employee stuck in the middle of it.

It's not trying to be deep. It's Bruce Campbell versus mutant shoppers in a toy store, and that's a perfect pick for the night after the big meal, when everyone's too full to sit through something heavy.

5. Home Sweet Home (1981)

Director: Nettie Peña · Starring Jake Steinfeld

One of the very first Thanksgiving slashers. An escaped killer, played by fitness guru Jake Steinfeld (yes, the "Body by Jake" guy), stalks a family gathered at a remote house for the holiday. It also marks Vinessa Shaw's first feature role.

Is it good? Not really. Is it a must for horror fans who want to say they've seen the whole Thanksgiving canon? Absolutely. Go in for the early-80s slasher weirdness and you'll have a great time.

6. ThanksKilling (2008)

Director: Jordan Downey · Budget: about $3,500

A foul-mouthed killer turkey hunts a group of college kids heading home for Thanksgiving break. Jordan Downey made it for a few thousand dollars and spoofs every low-budget horror cliché on purpose: the turkey is a handmade puppet, and the one-liners are terrible in the best way.

This is a group watch, not a solo watch. Put it on with friends, don't take it seriously for one second, and it might become a yearly tradition. It's already spawned sequels for people who can't get enough.

7. Blood Freak (1972)

Director: Brad F. Grinter · Starring Steve Hawkes

The strangest movie on this list, and that's saying something after a killer turkey puppet. A biker ends up on a turkey farm, eats experimental meat, and turns into a turkey-headed monster. It's famous for being a preachy anti-drug movie and a gore movie at the same time, with a narrator who keeps stopping to smoke and lecture you.

It's not technically set on Thanksgiving, but plenty of horror fans have made it a holiday tradition anyway. Watch it purely for the "how did this get made?" factor.

Bonus: Addams Family Values (1993)

Director: Barry Sonnenfeld · Starring Anjelica Huston, Raul Julia, Christina Ricci, Joan Cusack

Not a horror movie, but it has the greatest Thanksgiving scene in spooky movie history. Wednesday gets cast in the summer camp's cheery Thanksgiving pageant and goes completely off script, and the result is a deadpan, flaming takeover of the whole show. If you've got family in the room who won't sit through a slasher, this is your compromise pick.
